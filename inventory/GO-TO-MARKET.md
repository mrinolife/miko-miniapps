# Body Kitchen: launch and marketing system

## Category position
**The kitchen memory for people who forget what they bought.**

Do not lead with inventory management. That sounds like unpaid household administration. Lead with the moment: opening a full fridge, seeing no meal, and ordering delivery while produce dies behind a drawer.

## Brand directions to test

### 1. Body Kitchen
Best continuity with the current system. Warm, capable, broad enough for nutrition and recovery. Harder to own in search.

### 2. Stillgood
Memorable consumer promise. Flexible beyond neurodivergence. Requires trademark and domain checks before adoption.

### 3. Fridgebrain
The clearest ADHD-native concept and strongest social handle. More playful, possibly polarizing for a premium App Store product.

Working public name remains **Body Kitchen** until naming clearance and user testing. Tagline: **Remember your food. Eat what fits today.**

## Message pillars

### The closed-fridge problem
“If I can’t see it, it stops existing.” Show recognition in two seconds.

### Variable-energy cooking
A recipe app assumes you can cook. Body Kitchen asks what kind of effort is available and gives a meal that fits.

### Rescue without shame
The product catches food early enough for a small decision: eat, freeze, cook, or let it go.

### Less invisible admin
Photos, receipts, barcodes, and voice reduce manual inventory. Household sync stops duplicate buying and “I was saving that” conflicts.

## TikTok / Reels content engine

### Repeatable series
1. **Fridge blindness test**: show five items in a fridge, close it, ask viewers to name them, then show the app’s use-first queue.
2. **$27 rescue**: three foods nearing the decision point become one low-effort dinner. Use confirmed prices or label the amount as the grocery receipt total.
3. **What I can cook at 20% battery**: compare open-and-eat, assemble, and one-pan modes from the same kitchen.
4. **POV: your produce has object permanence now**: fast before/after capture demo.
5. **The leftovers archaeology department**: humorous container identification followed by a calm review flow.
6. **ADHD app design critique**: explain why streaks, seven-step entry, and 12 notifications fail; show the alternative.
7. **Build in public**: one weekly product decision, prototype change, or user correction. Keep data and kitchens anonymized.
8. **Can the camera understand this fridge?**: honest challenge clips where the model misses items and the review queue fixes them.

### Video format
0–2s: recognizable failure moment. 2–7s: product action. 7–18s: visible payoff. 18–24s: one honest boundary or miss. Final frame: waitlist or TestFlight CTA.

Shoot native vertical screen recordings plus real kitchen footage. Use captions. Avoid synthetic narrator voices and polished startup stock footage. The founder’s own kitchen and honest corrections are the asset.

## Creator strategy
Target small and mid-sized creators whose audience trusts their systems:
- ADHD and autistic adult creators
- low-energy cooking and disability-friendly kitchen creators
- food-waste and budget grocery creators
- meal-prep creators who teach flexible components rather than Sunday perfection
- dietitians who communicate without shame
- household organization creators willing to test whether capture reduces admin

Offer TestFlight access and a structured challenge. Pay for deliverables once a format proves conversion. Never require creators to disclose diagnoses or show private medication/health information.

## Other channels

### App Store Optimization
Initial keyword cluster: pantry inventory, fridge tracker, food expiry, leftovers, food waste, meal planner, recipes from ingredients, grocery inventory, ADHD meal planning. Validate volume and competition with an ASO tool before locking metadata.

Screenshot story:
1. “See what needs using.”
2. “Scan the shelf. Review the draft.”
3. “Choose the effort you have.”
4. “Cook from what is already home.”
5. “Share the kitchen, skip duplicate groceries.”

### Reddit and communities
Participate in ADHD, autism, meal planning, frugal, zero-waste, and cooking communities through transparent build logs and usability questions. Do not astroturf. Community rules and moderator approval come first.

### Search and evergreen content
Build high-intent pages around “recipes from what I have,” “ADHD meal planning,” “how to track freezer food,” and “leftover tracker.” Interactive tools can convert better than generic blog posts: shelf-life checklists, freezer label generator, and a no-login meal rescue tool.

### Partnerships
Pilot with ADHD coaches, occupational therapists, registered dietitians, university accessibility offices, food-waste nonprofits, and meal-kit adjacent brands. Clinical positioning is unnecessary; the product is a daily-living utility.

### Referral loop
A household invite is the natural referral. Both people receive one month of Plus after the invited member reviews their first shared capture. Add this after household sync is reliable.

## Launch stages

### Audience discovery
Publish problem-first content and a landing page with three concept demos. CTA: join design-partner group. Interview people who already tried pantry apps and stopped using them.

### Private alpha
20–40 kitchens. Weekly short check-in: what did the app save, what did it ask you to remember, what did you ignore? Collect correction data with consent.

### TestFlight season
Open 250–500 seats in cohorts. Run a seven-day “fridge memory” challenge without streak penalties. Creators demonstrate real captures and misses.

### App Store launch
Coordinate creator posts, Product Hunt as a secondary credibility event, email launch, Reddit build receipt, and Apple editorial pitch. The App Store page carries the conversion burden.

### Post-launch
Turn support questions into demos. Publish monthly product truth reports: recognition accuracy, correction patterns, processing costs, and the changes made. Keep personal food data out of public examples.

## Landing page draft

**Headline:** Your food should not disappear when the fridge closes.

**Subhead:** Body Kitchen remembers what is home, puts urgent food first, and finds a meal that fits the energy you have today.

**Primary CTA:** Join the first kitchens

**Proof sequence:**
- Scan a shelf or grocery haul.
- Confirm a short draft.
- See what needs a decision.
- Pick your effort level.
- Eat, freeze, cook, or clear it.

**Trust line:** Your kitchen data is private. Your photos are not training material. Export or delete everything in the app.

## Experiments before paid acquisition
- Problem framing: food waste vs decision fatigue vs grocery savings
- Name: Body Kitchen vs Stillgood vs Fridgebrain
- Demo: fridge scan vs use-first queue vs effort-based recipe
- CTA: waitlist vs design partner vs TestFlight
- Pricing: annual anchor and photo-scan limits

Track landing-page completion, qualified waitlist rate, TestFlight activation, and week-four retained kitchens. Do not buy broad install ads until capture-to-resolution retention works.

## Marketing safety and claims
- Do not promise a dollar amount saved without user-confirmed purchase data.
- Do not claim the app treats ADHD, autism, binge eating, or executive dysfunction.
- Do not imply food is safe solely because the app says so.
- Creator testimonials must disclose compensation and gifted access.
- Before public naming, perform trademark, domain, App Store, and social-handle clearance.
